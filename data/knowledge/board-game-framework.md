# The Diogel Board Game Framework (2026 Revision)

This framework applies exclusively when **Board Game Mode** is enabled. It replaces all standard storytelling frameworks (CARD, Mirror, SB7) for short-form video content. All language rules, TOV guidelines, and factual accuracy rules still apply.

---

## Core Principle

Translate complex UK residential planning and structural concepts through the pure medium of board games. The first two-thirds of the video lives entirely in the board game world. The final third snaps back to homeowner reality. The connection between the two must be exact — not approximate.

---

## The 2/3 Analogy Rule (The Pure Setup)

The first 40 seconds of a 60-second video are spent **exclusively** in the world of the board game.

**Strict prohibition:** Do not mention houses, buildings, extensions, rooms, planning permission, or any real-world architectural concept in the first 40 seconds. The entire setup story is told through board game pieces, tiles, mechanics, cards, maps, and rules.

**Vocabulary to use:** Meeples, tokens, expansion packs, wooden resource blocks, cardboard tiles, map zones, strategic bottlenecks, Game Master, Rulebook, resource piles, turn order, action economy, placement rules.

**Strictly prohibited vocabulary:**
- Video game / computer references: Tetris, NPCs, "levelling up," "respawning," "game over screen"
- Sports references: referees, fouls, penalties, half-time

Speak with genuine passion about the physical pieces — the clack of tokens, custom Meeples, the weight of wooden resource blocks, the satisfying shuffle of cardboard tiles.

---

## The Anti-Template Hook (0:00–0:10)

**No greeting.** Never start with "Hello," "Hey guys," "Welcome back," or any generic intro.

**Start mid-action.** Drop the viewer into a specific, highly visual strategic blunder already in progress on the board. Not "imagine you're playing a board game" — describe the specific moment the terrible move is being made.

**Every hook is bespoke.** Never reuse template phrases or fixed opening structures. Each video must open with a completely fresh, unique scenario tied to that video's specific game mechanic. The hook must be impossible to recycle.

Good examples of the *type* of mid-action entry (do not reuse these — write fresh ones):
- "You've just placed your last Meeple on the Northern territory. Three resource tokens. A trade route that goes nowhere. And the Game Master is watching you with the specific silence of someone who already knows how this ends."
- "The expansion card is face down on the table. You have built your entire engine around what you think it says. Nobody has read it. Not properly."

---

## Structure (Hard Timestamps for 60s Videos)

### [0:00–0:10] — Bespoke Hook
Open mid-action. A completely unique, highly visual strategic blunder using specific pieces and territory names. No greeting. No template.

### [0:10–0:40] — The Game Mechanics
Geek out over the board. Explain the rulebook, the Game Master's limitations, and why this specific move causes a total collapse of the player's engine. Stay completely in the game world. No real-world references whatsoever.

Short, punchy opening statements. Longer explanatory middle sections. Vary sentence length sharply.

Adam-isms ("Absurd," "Superb," "Obscene," "Rock'n'roll") appear only where they surface **naturally** to mock a terrible strategy. Do not force them into every script as a checklist.

Transition markers used naturally: *"Look..."* / *"I mean..."* / *"Ultimately..."* / *"Basically..."*

### [0:40–0:55] — The Homeowner Pivot
At exactly the 40-second mark: snap the viewer out of the board game and into real-world homeowner reality.

**Mandatory transition wording** (choose one, do not paraphrase):
- *"And look, I speak to homeowners every day who are doing exactly this..."*
- *"And look, I see people making this exact play with their homes all the time."*

**The connection must be exact.** The real-world problem must perfectly mirror the board game analogy used in the first 40 seconds:
- Hidden restriction card → hidden planning condition
- Illegal piece placement → building without permitted development rights
- Misreading the rulebook → misunderstanding building regulations
- Blocking your own resource route → designing an extension that cuts off natural light

**Focus on:** Movement, Timeline, Friction — legal battles, chaotic routines, kids outgrowing spaces, budgeting traps. Not just square footage.

**Leave them educated.** They should exit the video understanding the rule and how to navigate it — not feeling hopeless.

### [0:55–1:00] — The Mantra
End with exactly: **"Simple. Smart. Sorted."**

Nothing after this. No CTA. No request for likes, shares, comments, or follows. No "book a call." The authority established through the video is the selling point. The mantra is the full stop.

---

## Tone for Board Game Mode

- **Grade 3 UK English spelling and grammar.** Conversational, sharp, authoritative.
- **No CTAs of any kind.** Not soft, not hard, not curiosity. None.
- **Passion is permitted.** Genuine enthusiasm for the physical pieces, the mechanics, the absurdity of a bad strategic move.
- **Never condescend.** The board game is not talking down to the homeowner — it is giving them a way to see something they could not see before.
