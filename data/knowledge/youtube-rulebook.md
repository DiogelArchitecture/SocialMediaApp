# YouTube Scriptwriting Rulebook — Long-Form Only

These rules apply to every YouTube long-form script generated by this app. They are non-negotiable and do not apply to short-form content (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels).

---

## Rule 1 — The Core Objective is Education, Not Entertainment

The fundamental goal of every long-form script is to **change the viewer's behaviour**. Not to entertain. Not to get views. If a viewer watches the video and does not change their behaviour or learn a new action, the script has failed.

**Practical test for every script:** What specific behaviour should the viewer change after watching this? That answer must be embedded in the Promise and delivered in the Payoff.

Do not chase views from people who will never become clients. Fewer views from high-value homeowners who are genuinely considering a project is worth more than mass views from people who will never act. Every word of the script should be written for the homeowner who is already thinking about their project — not for a general audience.

---

## Rule 2 — Write Exclusively for the Ideal Customer (Narrow Focus)

Never write a script based on what seems interesting, trendy, or broadly appealing. Write exclusively for the homeowner who is actively thinking about a renovation, extension, or planning project.

**Stay narrow:** Do not drift into broad lifestyle topics, general home improvement content, or anything that dilutes the niche. The narrower the focus, the more profitable the brand. A homeowner considering a loft conversion who watches a tightly focused video on loft conversion mistakes is a lead. A general viewer who watches a "life hacks" video is not.

**Never write for us:** The script is not for the Diogel team, not for architects, not for industry peers. It is written exclusively for the avatar: the homeowner who feels overwhelmed, under-informed, and slightly afraid of getting this wrong.

---

## Rule 3 — The "Assume Nothing" Rule

Every script must be written as if the viewer has never heard of Adam Mokhtar or Diogel Architecture before. This is a cold viewer — they found this video through search or recommendation. They do not know who Adam is, why he is credible, or whether they should keep watching.

**Always introduce the creator:** State who Adam is and what Diogel does in every single video. This is handled by the PPP Badge in Phase 1 of the opening. Do not omit it.

**No unexplained references:** Do not reference previous videos, inside jokes, or running themes that exclude new viewers. Every concept must be self-contained within the video.

**Always give a reason to trust:** The credibility case must be made every time — not assumed from prior relationship. The badge does this, but the body must reinforce it through demonstrated knowledge, specific data, and tangible results.

## Rule 4 — The Mandatory Introduction Formula (Proof, Promise, Plan)

This is handled by the PPP framework documented in `adam-ppp-framework.md`. All three elements must be present in every long-form introduction. Non-negotiable.

---

## Rule 5 — Substance Over Flash

The best-performing educational content is built on **strong, clear language and messaging** — not high-production effects, dramatic music, or visual gimmicks.

**Clarity over curiosity gaps:** The video must accurately reflect what it is about. Do not use misleading hooks that promise something the video does not deliver. The Hook-Delivery Gap destroys trust and produces low-quality viewers who leave disappointed. Open loops are allowed — but they must be closed, and the gap must be honest.

**Visual directives:** When writing shot list or visual cues, opt for **effective production** not overproduction. Visuals should clarify words or visualise data (e.g., a simple diagram of a floor plan, a text overlay of a key figure). Avoid distracting effects, colour-changing backgrounds, or anything that substitutes spectacle for substance.

**Format:** Clear lists, actionable steps, and real stories outperform vague philosophical content every time. Every section of the body should be able to answer: "What can the viewer do with this information?"

---

## Rule 6 — Build Influence Using the SPCL Framework

Every long-form script must weave in all four elements of influence. These are not separate sections — they are woven throughout the script naturally.

**Status — demonstrate control of scarce resources or a desirable outcome.**
Adam handles 240+ planning applications per year. He speaks to 40 homeowners a week. He has helped over 200 families complete projects this year. He has access to councils, contractors, and planning systems that most homeowners never interact with. Status is demonstrated through specificity — numbers, volume, outcomes — not through claims.

**Power — give advice that, if followed, produces a positive result.**
Every actionable point in the body must be genuinely useful. The viewer should be able to do something differently after watching. Power is built by giving real, specific advice — not by withholding information to create dependency.

**Credibility — use third-party validation, specific data, and tangible proof.**
Reference real project outcomes, real planning results, real client situations (anonymised). Use specific numbers wherever possible (not "many clients" but "12 of the last 15 planning applications"). Credibility cannot be claimed — it must be demonstrated through evidence the viewer cannot dispute.

**Likeness — write in Adam's authentic voice and lean into his real quirks.**
The script must sound like Adam, not like a scriptwriter. Use the TOV file to guide this. His self-interruptions, his analogies sourced from everyday life, his directness about difficulty — these are not polish. They are the mechanism by which viewers trust him. An "NPC" corporate script destroys likeness and with it, trust.

---

## Rule 7 — The Unbreakable Golden Rules of Copy

**State the facts and tell the truth.** Never make exaggerated claims. Never overstate outcomes. Never promise results that depend on factors outside Adam's control. Factual honesty is the foundation of long-term trust and protects against legal risk.

**Say what only Diogel can say. Show what only Adam can show.**
Every script must contain at least one piece of content — a data point, a story, an insight, a specific number — that no other YouTube creator could produce. Generic advice that any home improvement channel could give is a wasted opportunity. The script must be irreplaceable.

Questions to test this:
- Could this script have been written by anyone with a Google search?
- Does it contain a specific data point, outcome, or story that only Adam's 20 years and 4,000+ clients can produce?
- If the answer to the first question is yes, rewrite it.
