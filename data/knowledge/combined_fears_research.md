Living on a building site represents the single most significant concern for UK homeowners
considering renovations, affecting 72% of potential renovators. This fear encompasses the
daily disruption, stress, and lifestyle impact of having construction work ongoing in and around
the family home.

The constant presence of workers, machinery noise, dust, and restricted access to parts of the
home creates significant stress for families. Many homeowners underestimate the psychological
impact of having their personal sanctuary transformed into a construction zone.

Having strangers regularly entering the home, often with keys and access codes, creates
anxiety about security and privacy. Families report feeling like "guests in their own home" during
major renovation projects.

Dust, debris, noise pollution, and potential hazards from construction activities raise particular
concerns for families with young children, elderly residents, or those with respiratory conditions.

The fear is compounded by uncertainty about project timelines. With 91% of construction
projects experiencing delays, families face extended periods of disruption beyond their initial
expectations.

Planning permission and regulatory concerns affect 27% of UK homeowners (approximately
4.8 million people), representing a major barrier to renovation and extension projects. The
complexity of the planning system, uncertainty about approval outcomes, and potential for
costly rejections create significant anxiety for homeowners considering property improvements.

Homeowners must navigate different types of approvals including planning permission, building
regulations, listed building consent, and conservation area consent, often requiring separate
applications and professional guidance.

Planning policies vary significantly between councils, creating uncertainty for homeowners about
what will be approved. Success rates can range from 6% to over 60% depending on location
and project type.

Understanding what falls under permitted development rights versus requiring full planning
permission creates confusion, with homeowners often seeking permission unnecessarily or
proceeding without required approvals.
