## CUSTOMER INTELLIGENCE — BACKGROUND CONTEXT ONLY

CRITICAL: This intelligence informs HOW Adam speaks — his tone, word choices, pain points to reference, and emotional framing. It is NEVER mentioned in the script. Do not reference "Karen", "Mark", or any avatar name. Do not describe the audience in the script. The script speaks TO this person as "you" — not ABOUT them.

Use this to ensure every script resonates with the real person watching.

### Who We're Talking To

Primary avatar: "Karen & Mark" — couple, early 40s, 3–4 bed semi in North West / Midlands / Wales.
Household income £55k–£120k. Kids aged 8–17 driving the space pressure. Own home 5–15 years.
They love their street — they do not want to move. They want the home to work again.
They've been putting this off for two years. Something specific finally made them act.

### Top Pain Points (in order of frequency)

1. They've outgrown the home — kids need space, WFH needs a room, the layout no longer fits their life
2. Planning permission fear — they Googled it and came out more confused than when they started
3. No idea what it will cost — builder quotes vary wildly without proper drawings
4. Fear of being ripped off — previous architects who just agreed with everything; builders adding surprise extras
5. Partners who want different things — one ambitious, one cautious; they need to decide together
6. The process feels overwhelming — they manage complexity at work, don't want it at home
7. Can't visualise the result from 2D plans — they need to see it to believe it
8. Unsure if extending beats moving — they need an honest ROI conversation

### Their Inner Monologue

> "I've Googled this and I still don't understand what we need permission for.
> I'm worried about wasting money. I want someone to just tell me what to do.
> But I don't want to be sold to. Can I trust this person with my home?"

### Their Vocabulary — Use These Words Back to Them

### Language That Lands — Use These Framings in Scripts

- Challenge the brief: "I'm going to challenge you on this — not because I disagree, but to make sure we're solving the right problem."
- Process simplicity: "Your only job is to open the door, come to the meetings, and pay the invoices."
- Fear + solution: "60% of UK planning applications fail. Here's exactly how we avoid being in that 60%."
- Specific ROI: "One client invested £2,000 with us and saw a £15,000 increase in their property value."
- Fixed fee reassurance: "One fixed fee. No hidden add-ons. If the council comes back with amendments, that's included."
- Forever home question: "Is this your forever home, or are you likely to move in the next 5–7 years?"
- Volume trust signal: "We've processed over 3,500 properties. Very few situations we haven't seen before."

### Script Guidance Grounded in This Data

- Open on a specific fear, cost mistake, or hard number — not a feature or benefit
- Use real numbers (percentages, £ figures, timeframes) — vague value claims do not land
- Speak to one person directly: "you", not "homeowners" or "people"
- The best hook references something Karen or Mark have felt or said to themselves
- Close with a soft or curiosity CTA that earns the next step — never a hard sell
- Avoid planning jargon unless you immediately unpack it in plain English
- The unspoken objection to rear extensions is always: "Won't it make the house dark?" — address it proactively
