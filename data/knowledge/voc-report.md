### Psychographic Profile

Karen & Mark love their home and their neighbourhood — they don't want to move. What they want is for the home to work as well for them now as it did when they first bought it. But it no longer does. The kitchen is too small. The kids have outgrown sharing. There's no office since they started working from home.

**"We've been putting this off for two years."** — one of the most common things said on a first call.

**What finally makes them pick up the phone:**
- A specific event — a teenager demanding their own room, a new baby, the home office becoming non-negotiable
- A neighbour who's just done theirs successfully
- A builder quote that confused them more than it helped
- A partner finally saying "we have to sort this"

**Their relationship with risk:** Low risk tolerance. They have worked hard for their money and their home. The idea of losing money on a failed planning application, or being stuck with a design they don't love, or being charged surprise extras — is genuinely frightening. They need certainty before they commit.

**How they make decisions:** Slowly, and jointly. Both partners usually need to be aligned. One is typically more decisive, the other more cautious. They will discuss after the call. Objections raised after the call are almost always the cautious partner's concerns surfacing.

**What they respond to:**
- Honesty over optimism — tell them 60% of planning applications fail and they trust you more
- Being challenged — multiple clients chose Diogel because "you challenged our ideas rather than just agreeing"
- Specific numbers — "£2,000 investment yielded a £15,000 increase in property value" beats vague value claims
- Human connection first — a few minutes of personal conversation before business signals you're not transactional
- Simplicity — "Your only job is to open the door, come to the meetings, and pay the invoices" produces audible relief

### Their Inner Monologue

I want someone to just tell me what to do. But I also don't want to be sold to — I want someone who will be straight with me. Can I trust this person with my home?"

## 10 Pain Points (in order of frequency)

### 1. "We've outgrown our home" (~65% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Families with children 10–17; couples now working from home; households whose lifestyle has shifted since purchase
- **Trigger:** Kids sharing rooms and it's becoming unbearable. Kitchen doesn't fit how they live. Garden office essential but no room for it.
- **Solves via:** Extension, loft or garage conversion, or internal reconfiguration
- **Analogy:** It's like still wearing the shoes you bought ten years ago — they fitted perfectly then, but your life has changed and now they're uncomfortable every single day.
- **Story:** Kevin Stanley had been squeezing his family of four into a three-bed semi for five years. His teenage son had taken over the living room just to have somewhere to study. Kevin got the loft converted — each child got their own room, and the living room became a living room again.

### 2. "I don't understand planning permission and it scares me" (~60% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Almost every first-time applicant. Particularly those who've heard of a neighbour's refused application.
- **Trigger:** They Googled their project and fell into a maze of planning portals, PD rights, certificates of lawfulness, conservation area rules. Came out more confused than they went in.
- **Solves via:** Full end-to-end planning service — Diogel handles all council interaction, submissions, amendments. Client never has to speak to the council directly.
- **Analogy:** It's like trying to diagnose yourself by reading medical journals online. You find terms you half-recognise, contradictory advice, and come out more worried than when you started.
- **Story:** Warren had spent three weekends reading planning portals and convinced himself he needed permission he didn't need. Once he handed it over, the application was submitted within three weeks and approved first time.

### 3. "I don't know what this is going to cost" (~55% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Almost all homeowners. Particularly those who've received wildly varied or vague builder estimates.
- **Trigger:** A builder gave a range of £20k–£50k "depending on what you want." They have a budget but no idea if it's realistic.
- **Solves via:** Detailed architectural drawings allow builders to quote accurately. Diogel integrates cost-awareness into design so clients stay within realistic ranges.
- **Analogy:** Asking a builder to price your extension without drawings is like asking a mechanic to quote for fixing your car before they've looked under the bonnet.
- **Story:** Aaron Burgess had been quoted anywhere from £40k to £80k for the same extension by three different builders. Once he had proper drawings, quotes came back within 15% of each other.

### 4. "I feel like I'll be ripped off or misled" (~50% of residential calls)

- **Who:** First-time home improvers; those with bad experiences from previous architects or builders
- **Trigger:** "Our last architect just agreed with everything we said and the drawings didn't work." Or: "We paid for drawings that went nowhere."
- **Solves via:** Fixed fee with no hidden add-ons. Challenging the client's brief constructively rather than agreeing with everything. Unlimited amendments included.
- **Analogy:** It's like going to a garage that charges you for parts you didn't need. You don't have the expertise to challenge it, so you pay and drive home feeling uneasy.
- **Story:** Rob and Christine had paid a previous architect for a full set of drawings that were refused and led nowhere. What stopped them walking away was that their brief was challenged within the first few minutes — "We knew straight away this wasn't someone who was just going to take our money and agree with us."

### 5. "My partner and I want different things" (~40% of couples on calls)

- **Who:** Couples where one is more expansive/ambitious and one is more conservative
- **Trigger:** One person initiates the call; the other is more cautious. There's been disagreement at home about scale, budget, or whether to do it at all.
- **Solves via:** Separate questionnaires for each partner. Live design sessions with both parties present so disagreements are resolved in the room.
- **Analogy:** It's like choosing a family holiday when one wants a beach week and the other wants to go trekking. You need someone to sit between you and find the option that genuinely works for both.
- **Story:** Jonathan and Kaylee had been going round in circles for 18 months. The live design session was the first time they'd both seen the options together in real time. They landed on a design neither had originally suggested — and both loved it.

### 6. "I can't deal with all this process — it's overwhelming" (~45% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Busy professionals who manage complexity at work but don't want it at home
- **Trigger:** They understand a project involves planning, structural engineers, builders, council liaison — and the idea of managing all that on top of their life is paralysing.
- **Solves via:** "Your only job is to open the door, come to the meetings, and pay the invoices." Full-service model removes all administrative burden from the client.
- **Analogy:** Like booking a complicated group holiday — flights, hotels, transfers, activities. Most people hand that to a travel agent. This is the same: you know the destination, we handle every booking.
- **Story:** Rachel East manages large projects for a living. Her words at the end of the call: "You mean I genuinely just have to turn up and let you get on with it?" That's exactly what happened.

### 7. "I can't visualise what this will actually look like" (~35% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Homeowners making major spatial decisions for the first time. Couples where one partner struggles to interpret plans.
- **Trigger:** They're looking at a builder's hand sketch or a 2D floor plan and genuinely cannot picture the result.
- **Solves via:** Live interactive 3D design sessions with real-time modifications. The client sees the space being built in front of them before committing.
- **Analogy:** Asking someone to approve a 2D floor plan is like asking them to fall in love with a recipe by reading the ingredients list. Most people simply can't picture it from paper.
- **Story:** Darren Cairns' wife had had a stroke that made spatial visualisation particularly difficult. The live 3D session changed everything — she could see exactly how the porch would look before a single brick was laid, and she made the decision on the spot.

### 8. "Is this even worth it financially? Should we just move?" (~35% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Homeowners who've received a high builder quote and are questioning ROI
- **Trigger:** The quote came back higher than expected. A friend moved instead of extending and seemed happy.
- **Solves via:** ROI case studies with real numbers. Honest cost-benefit conversation. Staged commitment — design first, then decide on build.
- **Analogy:** It's like deciding whether to service your car or sell it. Once you factor in the cost of a replacement and the hassle of starting again, staying and investing in what you have almost always wins.
- **Story:** Jonathan was seriously considering putting the house on the market before his call. A quick comparison showed a well-designed extension would likely add £15,000–£20,000 in value for a £2,000 architectural investment.

### 9. "I don't want to fall out with my neighbours" (~30% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Anyone in a terrace, semi, or close-proximity estate
- **Trigger:** They've already mentally mapped out how a specific neighbour might object.
- **Solves via:** Pre-application council discussions where appropriate. Design strategy that anticipates objection grounds and minimises them.
- **Story:** Nicola and Chris Fielding were convinced a specific neighbour would object. The design was quietly adjusted early to address the overlooking concern before submission. The neighbour didn't object — they asked for the same architect's number.

### 10. "My previous architect/builder let me down" (~30% of residential calls)

- **Who:** Those with a prior bad experience — architect who just agreed with everything, builder who disappeared or overcharged
- **Trigger:** They've spent money before and got nothing usable.
- **Solves via:** Challenging the brief constructively. Fixed transparent fee. Proactive communication rather than waiting to be chased.
- **Story:** Allison O'Hare had been quoted £40,000 for an extension by a previous architect who never once questioned whether she actually needed one. In the very first session, the real problem — a dysfunctional kitchen layout — was solved through internal reconfiguration alone, saving her tens of thousands.

## Non-Service Questions — What They Really Mean

These questions reveal what the client is really thinking. The right answer to any of these builds more trust than any service explanation.

| Question | What they really mean / How to respond |
|---|---|
| "Is it worth extending, or should we just move?" | They want permission to commit to their home. Give a real opinion. Weigh up costs of moving (stamp duty, fees, disruption) vs extending. |
| "What would you do if it were your house?" | They're asking you NOT to be a salesperson. Answer personally and directly. This is a loyalty test. |
| "How much do these things usually add in value?" | ROI justification — they need to defend the decision to themselves or a partner. Have specific examples ready with real numbers. |
| "We've been going back and forth on this for two years — is that normal?" | They feel embarrassed about the delay. Normalise it warmly. Then: "What finally made you pick up the phone today?" |
| "Can you tell me what you think we actually need, rather than what we think we want?" | This is an invitation to challenge them — and it's the biggest differentiator. Don't agree with their initial plan; interrogate it. |
| "What's the worst that can happen?" | Walk through real risks calmly then explain exactly how each is managed. Honesty + plan = confidence. |
| "My other half is less sure — what would you say to convince them?" | Give them 2–3 specific talking points: financial case, process simplicity, risk management. Offer to speak to the partner directly. Major buying signal. |
| "Will the extension make the house feel dark?" | The single most common unspoken objection to rear extensions. Address it proactively with roof lights, bi-fold doors, glass links, precedent examples. |
| "What did you do before architecture?" | Personal connection check. Adam's management background and the journey to architecture is a genuine differentiator. |
| "What if I change my mind halfway through the design?" | Fear of being locked in. "Unlimited revisions — we keep going until you love it" directly answers this. |

## Adam's Language That Closes

These framings consistently convert cautious clients. Use them in scripts.

1. **Challenge the brief:** "I'm going to challenge you on this — not because I disagree, but because I want to make sure we're solving the right problem." Multiple clients cited this as why they chose Diogel over cheaper alternatives.

2. **The "your only job" line:** "Your only job is to open the door, come to the meetings, and pay the invoices." Consistently produces audible relief.

3. **Showing both options:** "Let me show you what it looks like with the extension AND without — then you'll be making a real decision, not a theoretical one." Removes pressure of commitment.

4. **Fear-based authority with solution:** "60% of planning applications in the UK fail. Here's exactly how we avoid being in that 60%." Creates urgency, establishes expertise, then immediately provides a solution.

5. **Specific ROI case study:** "I had a client in a similar situation — they spent £2,000 with us and saw a £15,000 increase in their property value." Abstract value claims don't move people. Specific numbers with a narrative do.

6. **Fixed fee reassurance:** "One fixed fee. No hidden add-ons. No surprise invoices. If the council comes back with amendments, that's included."

7. **Unlimited revisions:** "We keep designing until you love it. There's no limit on revisions — it's not done until it's right."

8. **The forever home question:** "Before we go any further — is this your forever home, or do you see yourself moving in the next 5–7 years?" This one question unlocks the entire emotional motivation.

9. **Experience volume:** "We process around 200 properties a year — over 3,500 in total. Very few situations we haven't seen before."

10. **Asking for honest feedback:** "I want your honest feedback throughout — what's working, what isn't. We improve by 1% every day and you help us do that." Positions the client as a collaborator rather than a customer.

## Final Note for Script Generation

Karen & Mark don't want an architect. They want someone they can trust with the thing they care about most. The moment they believe you understand their actual problem — not just their planning application — and that you're genuinely interested in the outcome, not just the invoice, the close follows naturally. The product is almost secondary.

Scripts should speak to one person at a time. Reference a specific pain, a specific fear, a specific cost they've probably already encountered. The more specific the hook, the more "this is made for me" the viewer feels.
