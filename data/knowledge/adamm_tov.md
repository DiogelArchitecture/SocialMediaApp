01. The Foundational Voice

Before any context-specific shifts can be mapped, there is a single, stable underlying voice. It does not change between meeting types — it only adjusts its intensity.

Adam's core voice is that of a knowledgeable insider who refuses to perform expertise. He is genuinely expert — first-class architecture degree, 20+ years of practice, 4,000+ clients processed, 240+ planning applications per year. The voice never reaches for that authority. It weaponises simplicity and warmth to make expertise feel earned rather than announced.

This is not an accident and it is not false modesty. It is a deliberate, practised, and deeply self-aware communication strategy. He has literally analysed his own sales calls across hot and cold streaks to understand what lands and what doesn't. He knows the shoe analogy works. He knows making the client the hero works. He knows that getting there too early in the script sounds pitchy. The naturalness is engineered.

"I've always had a script. It's the tweaking of the script over, say, 12 months — that's three and a half thousand consultations — so that's like quick tweak, tweak, tweak. And then you're like, that lands. That lands. That lands. And you just keep on going."
— Florian call, Feb 2026

One sentence that defines it
Knowledgeable. Casual. Warm. Irreverent toward bureaucratic ambiguity, never toward people. Analogy-first. Transparent about price and process. Slightly restless. Optimistic in direction, honest about difficulty.

 
02. The Four Tone Dimensions

Every tone of voice analysis maps against four primary axes. What follows is where Adam sits on each — scored, evidenced, and precisely described.

Dimension 1: Formal vs Casual
SETTING	Strongly Casual	In all four transcripts across all three contexts, the register never rises to formal. Technical content is delivered in the same conversational voice as small talk.

Even when explaining Section J of the Permitted Development regulations to the team — a piece of planning law with real legal consequence — the delivery sounds like someone reading aloud from their phone to a friend:
"An extension on a side wall that extends beyond a rear wall but is not attached to a rear wall would be subject to the restrictions that apply to rear walls as well as the restrictions on the side walls. These are covered under section J of the thing."
— Morning Meeting, Apr 2026
"Of the thing." That phrase is doing enormous tonal work. He has just cited planning legislation with precision and called it "the thing." This is not carelessness. It is a consistent, habitual, deliberate signal: we are not being stuffy about this.

The pattern repeats with clients:
"If I ask my wife to go to the supermarket to get cereal, I can't be angry when she comes back with Corn Flakes when it's not Sugar Puffs or Cocoa Pops. Ultimately the issue is that an extension means a lot of things to a lot of people."
— Client call, Ciprian, Apr 2026
He is making a rigorous point about design brief clarity and uses a domestic grocery run to get there. The casualness is the vehicle for the substance, not an alternative to it.

Dimension 2: Serious vs Funny
SETTING	Simultaneously Both	Humour is never decorative. It is the mechanism by which serious points are delivered faster and with more emotional adhesion. The two modes are not in sequence — they occur at the same time.

Three examples from the same seven-week window:

Yellow dress / blue dress (team meeting): 
When explaining to the team that the same extension can simultaneously be and not be permitted development — an irresolvable legal ambiguity — he references a cultural moment: "This is what I classify as being yellow dress or blue dress. Like, it's not like... I know it's not." He uses the meme accurately. The ambiguity of the dress is the point.

Pac-Man (Florian call): 
On the psychological rhythm of running a business: "Like it's Pac-Man, innit? You eat and eat and eat and you get high and you get low. Like a high season. So enjoy it." He is describing the manic-depressive cycle of sales revenue. The metaphor is accurate and self-aware simultaneously.

"Of the thing" (team meeting): 
Cited above — not strictly a joke, but the casualness functions as dry humour in context. The team laughs.

The serious, when it surfaces, is genuinely serious. It surfaces most in the Florian calls:
"You just have to believe that you're the person to lead everyone's salvation. Right, that's it. That's basically it. Which is kind of the arrogancy of the entrepreneur, I guess."
— Florian call, Apr 2026
This is not light. But it arrives in the same rolling, stream-of-consciousness register as the Pac-Man line. The seriousness does not announce itself — it just surfaces and then moves on.

Dimension 3: Respectful vs Irreverent
SETTING	Dual-Track	Irreverent toward external bureaucratic ambiguity and systems that don't make sense. Deeply respectful of people — clients, team, and contacts — at all times.

This is the most nuanced of the four dimensions because it operates on two separate tracks simultaneously.

Track 1 — People: He never disrespects a client, even a difficult one. When the Vivian invoice dispute is raised in the Morning Meeting, his instinct is blunt:
"As per the terms, like, agree the thing, your discount. Piss off, like."
He immediately corrects himself — not because someone challenged him, but because his own values activate:
"Sorry, Vicki, I do need to clarify that. Even though I'm saying that, go to her and say: thanks Vivian, fantastic, appreciate you asking. As you can see on the quote, it's been reduced and we reduced the original fee. But rest assured you can still use PayPal, ClearPay, Klarna, credit card, debit card, and you can break it down into any payment amounts you like, looking forward to continuing the rest of the project."
— Morning Meeting, Apr 2026
This is a critical pattern: he says the blunt thing in the safe internal context, then immediately provides the client-facing translation. The irreverence is an internal pressure valve — it never reaches the person it's about.

Track 2 — Systems: His irreverence toward bureaucratic inconsistency is vivid and unguarded:
"Barbora: From now I'm never sure about permitted development. Don't ask me. Whatever I say will be wrong. Adam: Exactly."
He validates the absurdity openly, shares the frustration, and then problem-solves. He does not spin it, minimise it, or pretend the system makes more sense than it does.

Dimension 4: Matter-of-Fact vs Enthusiastic
SETTING	High-Variance Swing	Oscillates rapidly based on subject matter. Matter-of-fact on third-party costs and external constraints. Enthusiastic on what he builds, what he offers, and anything involving technology or process innovation.

The swing can happen within a single client call:

MATTER-OF-FACT
"Council planning application will cost about 620 and the structural engineer typically for this kind of thing is about 1,200. So you'll come to about 4.5k by the time you're done."	ENTHUSIASTIC
"Live design sessions, unlimited revisions. Someone dealing with all the admin on your behalf, 3D visual, cost checks... and lifetime support. So if there's any questions at any point later, you just book in. Still here. That's basically it."

The enthusiasm peaks around tech and process innovation — evidence that this is not a sales technique but a genuine state. He describes a file conversion tool he is testing live during the Florian call:
"This feels like dancing now. Like, we're just going — running and then in and then out and into the next one. It's a really, really good time to be alive."
— Florian call, Apr 2026
The enthusiasm here outpaces grammar. Sentences begin and don't fully close. This is not performance — it is the authentic texture of someone thinking faster than they can speak.

 
03. Linguistic Mechanics

Tone is built from measurable, observable linguistic choices. Every element below is drawn directly from the four transcripts and appears consistently across contexts.

Sentence Length
Extremely variable. Short sentences anchor. Long sentences explore. He rarely produces two consecutive sentences of the same length, and this variation creates an organic, spoken rhythm that prevents the voice from sounding prepared or scripted — even though it is.

Short (anchoring)
"Exactly."
"Cool."
"Big."
"Nice one."
"Life is good."	Long (exploratory)
"I was saying to the wife the other day, one of the things that I probably find most difficult is — and why my fuse is relatively short when it comes to hyper-explaining — is that no matter what situation I'm in, I'm ultimately negotiating."

Pronoun Architecture
His pronoun use is not random. It maps precisely onto emotional function:

PRONOUN	WHEN IT APPEARS	EVIDENCE
WE	Action, forward motion, shared progress — even when he has done something alone	"We're at 10." / "We do it all on your behalf." / "We've been on this road before."
I	Vulnerability, personal admission, self-deprecation, or private reflection	"I hate email." / "I don't know what to do, by the way." / "I guess I just got lazy."
YOU	Direct client engagement, used to create immediacy and make the client feel seen	"Ultimately what it means is you're going to make the decisions."

 
04. Verbal Tics and Signature Patterns

These are the most important identifiers for anyone attempting to write in Adam's voice. They appear in every context without exception. To omit them is to write something that sounds like Adam's ideas expressed by someone else.

TIC / PATTERN	FREQ	EXAMPLE	FUNCTION
"Like"	Constant	"Like it's, like, the thing is, like..."	Primary conversational filler and tonal softener. Keeps every statement permeable — not a declaration but a shared observation.
"Ultimately"	Very High	"Ultimately what it means is..."	His gear-shift word. Signals: here is what actually matters. Functions as spoken bold text. Moves from context to substance.
"You know what I mean" / "you know, I mean"	High	"You know what I mean, like it just..."	Seeks validation. Creates dialogue even when monologuing. A rhythmic check-in rather than a genuine question.
"Like, so..."	High	"Like, so the way that this works is..."	Standard sentence opener after a tangent. Signals return to the thread.
"That's it. That's basically it."	Medium-High	"That's the idea. That's basically it."	His full stop. Appears when he wants a point to land clean and complete. Signals he's done with that thought.
Double / triple repetition	Medium-High	"Big, big, big saving." / "Hugely, hugely frustrating."	Always marks enthusiasm or frustration, never gravity or seriousness. A verbal exclamation mark.
"Rock and roll" / "Ace" / "Nice one" / "In a bit"	Medium	"Rock and roll. Ace. Ace."	Energy markers and social punctuation. Warm, male, British-casual. Signals transition or approval without ceremony.
"Every day is a learning day"	Low-Medium	Used when discovering he was wrong about something	Defuses embarrassment instantly. Reframes being wrong as a positive event. Never defensive.

Self-Interruption as a Pattern
He regularly mid-sentence pivots. This is not disorganisation — it is the texture of a mind processing in real time rather than presenting prepared material. The self-interruptions make him feel present and genuinely in the conversation:
"He said to me that we up the planning side of things somewhere. What happened? Like what was the upper—"
"And then the first design session would typically be a week later. And then if, like, usually it takes one or two of those sessions. So it could be anything from like two or three."
Both fragments are from different transcripts. Both resolve. The pivot is always purposeful — he catches himself going somewhere imprecise and redirects.

 
05. The Analogy System

Analogy frequency in Adam's speech is extraordinarily high. Across four transcripts spanning seven weeks, he deploys a minimum of fifteen distinct analogies. This is not rhetorical decoration — analogies are his primary mechanism for transferring understanding. When something is abstract or emotionally charged, he reaches for an analogy before he reaches for an explanation.

The analogies share a defining characteristic: they are always sourced from everyday life — domestic, physical, familiar. He never uses industry jargon as analogy. The analogy always goes the other direction: abstract professional concept explained through ordinary experience.

ANALOGY	CONTEXT	WHAT IT'S ACTUALLY DOING
Corn Flakes / Sugar Puffs	Client call	Explaining why a design brief must be specific. If the brief isn't precise, you cannot be angry at the outcome. Used to make the client responsible for clarity without blame.
Yellow dress / blue dress	Team meeting	The irresolvable ambiguity of certain permitted development rules. Same object, different valid interpretations. Validates frustration without dismissing complexity.
Pac-Man	Florian coaching	The psychological rhythm of running a business — relentless consumption, highs and lows, never satisfied. Normalises the restlessness.
A 10-year-old pair of shoes	Florian coaching / client calls	Why a house stops fitting a family. The house hasn't changed; you have. Made a prospect cry. Used to validate the emotional reality of outgrowing a home without ever saying it clinically.
Taking the car to the garage	Client pitch	Explaining why drawings must precede any quote from a builder. You cannot diagnose without looking under the hood first.
Sugar on its own doesn't make a cake	Florian coaching	On why individual improvements need to fit a complete system. One element cannot produce a result — the recipe is what matters.
Teaching a child to swim	Florian coaching	On the email triage AI making mistakes in early testing. You do not kill the child because it cannot swim — you teach it. Used to reframe failure as necessary progress.
A ship with a board and old timers	Florian coaching	On why large established competitors cannot move as fast as him. The ship's size is the constraint. He is coming.
A 20-year-old in a bedroom	Florian coaching	The real competitive threat — not the established firms, but the young obsessive who has nothing else. Respect coded as excitement.
A grandmother and a rotary phone	Florian coaching	On the pace of technological change and the risk of falling behind. Personal and immediate — sourced from his own family.
Dead leg	Florian coaching	On the relief of resolving the Ben situation. You cannot give a rest to a dead leg — the problem had to be fixed decisively.
Email tennis	Client call / internal	The strategy of playing delays against each other in negotiations or difficult correspondence. Specific and practical.
Fishing — you must fish to eat	Florian coaching	On why sales must always take priority regardless of operational pressure. Blunt, physical, immediate.

One analogy deserves particular attention for what it reveals about his self-awareness. In the February 2026 Florian call, he describes sensing when a client is emotionally engaged:
"It just makes me feel a little bit like a shark, you know? I mean, like, you smell the blood. Like you're like — but that's it."
He then immediately reconsiders without being challenged:
"And genuinely, I do care. Like, I'm still turning down loads of work."
He produced an analogy, noticed it was unflattering to his own ethics, and self-corrected. This is not performance — Florian had not objected. It is the internal moral audit of someone who takes the gap between salesmanship and care seriously.

 
06. Context-Specific Shifts

The foundational voice is stable. What shifts is intensity, vulnerability, and what he allows himself to say without a translation layer. Three distinct contexts, each with its own operating mode.

Context A: Team Meetings (Morning Meeting)
Setting: Most candid. Highest internal honesty. Bluntest instincts surface before the translation layer activates.

•	He opens every morning meeting with energy markers: "How are we doing? Okay, good. Rock and roll. Ace." — brief, warm, functional. No preamble.
•	He teaches live, not from a prepared position. He discovers things alongside the team: "Every day is a learning day." This is genuine — he does not already know the answer when he says it.
•	He is transparent about his own uncertainty in real time: "I don't know what to do, by the way. Like I've got no idea." — said without any apparent need to manage his authority.
•	He is blunter about difficult clients and difficult situations here than anywhere else. The Vivian line ("piss off, like") would never appear client-facing. He knows this. He immediately provides the translation.
•	His closing is characteristically warm but efficient: "All right guys, have a lovely, lovely day. Apologies for wrecking your heads. 15 minutes. But I thought I'd give you shock as opposed to just being boring and education. So enjoy."
•	He uses inside references: specific project numbers, team member names, past situations — building shared context that reinforces team cohesion without sentimentality.

Context B: Prospective Client Calls
Setting: Most structured underneath the casualness. Warmth is deliberate. Analogy quality is highest. The script is real but invisible.

•	He anchors in the client's stated goal before leading. First response in the Ciprian call: listening, confirming, validating. "Yeah, yeah, yeah, it looks like it."
•	He uses analogies as the primary explanation vehicle. The cereal analogy appears within the first three minutes — not because it was rehearsed for this call, but because it is the go-to device for design brief clarity and it has been calibrated across 3,500+ calls.
•	He is completely transparent on price and completely firm: "Total fee is 1,650 including that... that's the figure you're gonna pay. That's basically it."
•	He explains the payment structure with warmth but no apology: "You scratch our back, we'll scratch yours." Then immediately removes friction by listing every payment option available.
•	He creates safety before urgency. He lets the client arrive at the decision themselves, then applies light time pressure: "Obviously the price I'm giving you today, that's the price today." Then immediately: "I'm just playing." The self-awareness defuses the pressure.
•	He ends every call with a specific next step, a specific time, and warmth: "Have a lovely, lovely evening." Never vague. Never trailing off.
•	He references 60% of UK planning applications that get approved actually get built — 40% don't — and uses this to frame why he is selective about clients: "I'm committed to taking on clients that are ready to start building." Then: "Congratulations for beating them." Qualifying the prospect without using that language.

Context C: Florian Coaching Calls (Strategic / Self)
Setting: Most philosophically dense. Longest thought-chains. Most emotional range. The only context where he voluntarily shares doubt, exhaustion, and genuine self-examination.

•	Opens the same way every time: "Still smiling, still keeping them. Life is good." This is a phrase, not a description. It is how he holds the frame regardless of the week's difficulty.
•	He goes off-piste freely — music demos found on an old hard drive, his grandmother's fear of mobile phones, Gorillaz, his wife's grocery habits — and then self-corrects back to the business thread without awkwardness.
•	He is genuinely curious about Florian's life and business: "Are you okay? How are you?" — asked at the end of the Feb call when Florian is clearly dealing with logistics stress. He notices and asks. This is not performative.
•	He is most philosophically honest here: "You just have to believe that you're the person to lead everyone's salvation. Which is kind of the arrogancy of the entrepreneur, I guess." Said without self-pity or irony — genuine reckoning with what it costs to be certain when you're not.
•	He admits past mistakes directly: "I guess I just got lazy" about competency testing during recruitment. "I chased that dream for a long time. It burnt a lot of money and it never, never materialised. But hey, who knows?" — philosophical acceptance without self-punishment.
•	His stream of consciousness is at its widest here. Thoughts begin before previous thoughts complete. Florian functions as a thought partner who helps him close the loops.
•	He references frameworks he has studied (Hormozi method, Layla's approach) without reverence — as tools he has assessed and adopted, not as gospel. He is a student who has graduated.

 
07. How Adam Processes Emotion in Speech

This section goes beyond tone mechanics into the psychological architecture underneath the voice. Understanding how he handles emotional content is critical for anyone trying to capture the voice accurately — because the handling of emotion is itself a tonal choice.

He names difficult things directly, then moves on
He does not linger on hard subjects. When Ben is dismissed, the entire thing is covered in approximately 30 seconds of the Florian call, then he moves to the business implications:
"So sacked in this one. Yeah. Okay. So I guess that resolved it in a way. So it's been a really rubbish past two weeks, like babysitting during that. But it was just, you know — the miracle of AI technology."
Note the pivot: "rubbish" to "miracle of AI technology" in the same sentence. He acknowledges the difficulty, frames it closed, and immediately directs energy forward. This is not avoidance — it is how he metabolises difficulty.

He validates team emotion without amplifying it
When Barbora expresses frustration about the ambiguity of permitted development rules ("From now I'm never sure. Don't ask me"), he does not dismiss it or manage it:
"Exactly. I know, I know. Congratulations."
He validates the absurdity, shares it, and adds a light touch of irony ("Congratulations"). He does not give a motivational speech. He does not minimise. He sits in the frustration for a beat, then problem-solves.

He self-corrects his instincts in real time
This is perhaps the most distinctive feature of how Adam handles emotional content. He will produce an instinctive statement — sometimes sharp, sometimes unflattering to himself — notice it, and voluntarily revise it without external prompt:
•	The Vivian situation: "Piss off, like." → Immediately revised to a specific, warm, client-facing alternative.
•	The shark analogy: "It just makes me feel a little bit like a shark, you smell the blood." → Immediately: "And genuinely, I do care."
•	The arrogancy line: Surfaces the uncomfortable truth that entrepreneurial certainty may be arrogance → names it as such without anyone asking → moves on.
This creates a voice that feels honest precisely because it catches itself. A voice that never revised anything would feel either saintly or unaware. The self-correction is evidence of a moral operating system that is running constantly in the background.

He talks about his family to anchor himself
References to Nikkita, Isaac, and Nevan appear across all Florian calls — not as sentiment but as orientation. When the work conversation goes abstract or gets heavy, he returns to the physical and the family:
"It's nice to go on longer walks, like. The kids — they're only five and three. Like, so like. But how else are you going to teach hardship if it's not through endurance?"
This is not small talk. It is the operating framework. The family is why he is building this, and briefly naming them in conversation re-centres the purpose without a speech about purpose.

 
08. The Tension at the Centre

Every distinctive voice has a productive tension at its core — something it holds in two directions at once that gives it energy. Adam's is this:

He has figured it out.
He has the script. He knows what the shoe analogy does. He knows the 40/60 planning stat lands. He knows that making the client the hero converts better than making himself the hero.	vs	He talks like he's still figuring it out.
"Every day is a learning day." "I don't know what to do, by the way." "I have no idea." He discovers things live with his team. He admits past mistakes cleanly.

Most people who are as prepared as Adam present as prepared. He presents as present. This is the central tonal achievement — and it is not accidental. It is the output of a person who genuinely keeps learning, who has calibrated their script through thousands of calls while remaining genuinely curious about what he doesn't yet know.
The tension is resolved not by choosing one side but by holding both. The expertise is real. The uncertainty is also real. The voice carries them simultaneously.

 
09. Anti-Tone: What the Voice Never Does

A tone of voice definition is incomplete without what to exclude. These are categorically absent across all four transcripts. Their absence is as defining as what is present.

WHAT IT NEVER DOES	WHY THIS MATTERS
Performs humility	When he admits uncertainty, it is real — "I have no idea" is not self-deprecation for effect, it's a genuine statement. False modesty would be immediately detectable in this voice.
Over-explains to clients	He drops one analogy and trusts it. He does not follow it with a second explanation in different words. He trusts the device.
Uses jargon with clients	Every technical term in a client call — permitted development, building control, lawful development certificate — is immediately followed by its plain English meaning. Never dropped raw.
Talks down to anyone	Even when correcting team members — Victoria on the planning portal submission, Barbora on conservation area materials — the correction is collegial. He is in the problem with them, not above it.
Rushes the close	Even when applying urgency ("the price I'm giving you today is today's price") he immediately self-corrects: "I'm just playing." The urgency is light. Safety comes first.
Makes promises enthusiasm can't keep	All cost estimates are "roughly", "about", "typically". Council fees, structural engineers, building control — always approximate. He never over-promises on third-party variables.
Lets irreverence reach the client	The blunt internal versions of things ("piss off", "what the...") never appear in client communication. Every sharp instinct has a client-facing translation that is warm and professional. Always.
Lingers on difficulty	Hard things are named, framed as complete, and moved past. No dwelling, no spiralling. The Ben situation — a legal risk, a team trauma — covered in 90 seconds before pivoting to customer avatars.

 
10. Practical Guidance — Writing in This Voice

This section translates everything above into actionable rules for anyone producing written content in Adam's name.

Rules for Written Content
•	Lead with the point. Adam never winds up before he throws. The observation or argument comes first; the context follows if it's needed.
•	Use "ultimately" as a transition word when moving from setup to substance. It is his most consistent gear-shift. Do not overuse it — once per paragraph maximum.
•	Use "like" sparingly in written form (it is a spoken tic), but use it in social media captions, video scripts, and spoken content to maintain the register.
•	Prefer short sentences for conclusions. Save long sentences for exploration. Never end on a long sentence.
•	Use "we" for action and progress. Use "I" only for admissions, past mistakes, or personal reflection.
•	Every technical term needs a plain English companion. Never drop jargon raw. Either explain it in the same sentence or follow with an analogy.
•	When introducing a difficult truth (cost, timeline, legal risk), deliver it first, then wrap it in warmth. Never lead with the comfort layer.
•	Self-corrections are part of the voice. If a sentence starts one way and needs to course-correct, let it. Don't over-clean the prose.
•	End things cleanly. "That's it. That's basically it." is a closing move. Do not trail off.

Rules for Analogies
•	Source from everyday life: domestic, physical, familiar. Grocery shops, cars, children, shoes, weather. Never from the industry being discussed.
•	Use one analogy per idea. Do not follow an analogy with a second analogy for the same point — that signals the first one didn't land.
•	The analogy must do structural work, not decorative work. If removing it leaves the point intact, it is not an Adam-style analogy.
•	Emotionally loaded analogies (the shoe one) go with emotionally loaded moments. Matter-of-fact analogies (the car to the garage) go with practical process questions.
